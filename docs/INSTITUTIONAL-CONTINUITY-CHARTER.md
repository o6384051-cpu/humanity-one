# HUMANITY ONE — Institutional Continuity & Stewardship Charter

**Status:** Draft for public discussion; not yet legally adopted  
**Applies to:** The HUMANITY ONE mission, its assets, data, services, and any future legal entity or operating partners.

> This document records governance principles. It does not appoint a successor, transfer ownership, create a trust or legal entity, or override applicable law. Before adoption, it must be reviewed and approved through a lawful process in the jurisdiction where the organization is established.

## 1. Mission comes before any individual

HUMANITY ONE exists to help people improve their lives through trustworthy access to education, dignified work, small-enterprise opportunities, and humanitarian support.

The mission must be delivered without discrimination based on race, colour, religion, nationality, language, sex, disability, or social status. Essential services should remain free wherever reasonably possible. The project must never publish fabricated opportunities, beneficiaries, partners, outcomes, or financial figures.

The founder's role is stewardship, not permanent personal control. No person—including the founder—may privately appropriate charitable assets, beneficiary data, or the project's identity.

## 2. Continuity if the founder is unavailable

The project must not depend on one person's continued availability, memory, devices, or accounts.

Before the project is treated as institutionally resilient, its governing body should establish:
- A documented succession procedure for temporary absence, incapacity, resignation, or death.
- At least three independent, vetted stewards where lawful and practical; no single steward should be able to unilaterally control funds, production credentials, and beneficiary data.
- A written method to appoint, replace, suspend, and remove stewards, with recorded reasons and conflict-of-interest checks.
- A continuity contact channel and a secure, regularly tested handover package.
- A process for resolving deadlock and escalating suspected misconduct to an independent reviewer.

No successor is named by this draft. Appointment must follow the adopted governance rules and applicable law, not informal promises or access to a password.

## 3. Separation of powers and financial safeguards

- Separate governance oversight, day-to-day operations, financial approval, and technical administration as far as resources permit.
- Require two-person approval for material transfers, changes to financial control, disposal of significant assets, or changes to production access.
- Keep project funds separate from personal funds and accounts.
- Publish periodic income, expenditure, fees, commissions, balances, and supporting evidence, while redacting personal and security-sensitive information.
- Any optional referral or service revenue must be used for the mission, service continuity, and responsible development—not private profit.
- Disclose conflicts of interest, related-party transactions, compensation, and procurement decisions.
- Never promise donations, aid, jobs, or grants that have not been verified and authorized.

If the project cannot yet establish a lawful separate entity or bank account, it must disclose the interim arrangement and its limitations; it must not imply that informal arrangements provide legal asset protection.

## 4. Independent organizations and public trust

Partner organizations retain their independence. HUMANITY ONE must not describe an organization as a partner until its identity, authorization, and written consent have been verified.

Beneficiary consent to receive contact or submit a request is not a partnership agreement and is not blanket consent to publish personal information.

Public reporting should distinguish clearly between:
- Verified facts;
- Information supplied by a third party but not independently verified;
- Proposals or plans not yet implemented;
- Estimates and limitations;
- Confirmed outcomes supported by evidence.

Corrections should be dated, explained, and preserved in an auditable change history. Do not silently rewrite material public claims.

## 5. Beneficiary dignity, privacy, and data stewardship

- Collect only data necessary to provide a clearly explained service.
- Obtain specific, informed consent before sharing personal data with a partner or publishing a beneficiary story.
- Never make aid conditional on publicity, testimonials, referrals, or unnecessary data collection.
- Apply least-privilege access, account isolation, secure authentication, encryption appropriate to the sensitivity of the data, retention limits, and documented deletion procedures.
- Keep production secrets out of source control, issue comments, logs, and public backups.
- Maintain a documented incident response process: contain, investigate, preserve evidence, notify affected people and authorities when legally required, and record corrective action.
- Provide a clear way for people to access, correct, or request deletion of their data, subject to lawful retention obligations.

A successful build, passing static test, or encrypted archive alone does not prove that the service is secure or recoverable.

## 6. Technical continuity and recoverability

The stewards should maintain an inventory of repositories, domains, hosting projects, databases, object-storage buckets, third-party services, billing dependencies, and accountable owners.

For each critical system:
- Keep at least two encrypted backups in separate locations, with encryption keys stored separately.
- Document recovery steps without publishing credentials or secret values.
- Test restoration periodically in isolated, disposable environments—not against production.
- Record the date, scope, result, limitations, and responsible reviewer for each test.
- Maintain a documented process to rotate credentials and revoke access when a steward leaves.
- Ensure more than one authorized steward can perform recovery, using controlled access and auditable approvals.
- Prefer open standards and portable exports to reduce dependence on a single vendor.

A backup is not considered proven until a restoration test and application-level checks have succeeded. A partial export must be labelled partial.

## 7. Ethical operation and accountability

All automated decisions that affect access to assistance, opportunities, or eligibility must have a human review and an appeal route. AI must not fabricate facts, impersonate people, conceal uncertainty, or make unreviewable high-impact decisions.

The project should publish:
- Its current governance and steward roles;
- How to report fraud, abuse, conflicts, or privacy concerns;
- How complaints and corrections are handled;
- What services are actually available and in which locations;
- Funding sources and the use of project funds;
- Known limitations, outages, and material incidents.

Retaliation against good-faith whistleblowers or people reporting mistakes is prohibited.

## 8. Amendment, review, and adoption

This draft should be reviewed at least annually and whenever there is a material change in law, organizational structure, funding, or data risk.

Adoption requires:
1. Legal review in the applicable jurisdiction;
2. Publicly recorded approval by the properly authorized governing body;
3. A named version, effective date, and change history;
4. Publication of the adopted text and the process for future amendments.

Changes to the mission, asset-lock principles, privacy commitments, or non-discrimination rule should require a heightened approval threshold defined in the organization's lawful governing documents.

## 9. Immediate implementation checklist

- [ ] Identify the current legal owner/operator of each project asset and service.
- [ ] Inventory all production systems and the people who can access them.
- [ ] Establish an independent interim oversight group with written terms and conflict disclosures.
- [ ] Draft lawful succession and asset-transfer documents with qualified local counsel.
- [ ] Separate project finances from personal finances and publish the interim accounting method.
- [ ] Complete a data inventory, retention schedule, privacy notice, and incident-response procedure.
- [ ] Test backups and restoration in an isolated environment and record evidence.
- [ ] Publish a verified register of organizations; mark unverified contacts as unverified.
- [ ] Review this charter publicly and record comments, decisions, and unresolved risks.
